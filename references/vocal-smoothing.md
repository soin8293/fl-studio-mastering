# Smooth vocals from processed stems

Use when a vocal needs a softer, finished character while retaining clarity, especially when supplied as a processed stem over a finished instrumental.

## Establish the source

Confirm stem roles and common export origin, preserve source copies and place them at the same start. Retain a longer vocal/reverb tail rather than trimming to the beat's length. Use a compatible FL template and confirm separate inserts and one intended route to Master.

Inspect the existing processing and measure each stem. Exported tuning, distortion, compression, reverb or EQ cannot be removed by bypassing the new FL insert. An already-dense export may need less processing. True peaks above 0 dBFS alone do not establish hard-clipped samples. Spectrum peaks are candidates to investigate, not proof that a frequency should be cut; strong harmonics can belong to the voice or instrument.

## Build a restrained comparison

1. Inventory the user's presets and their actual active stages. Identify the component used; do not say a whole artist preset was applied when only its EQ or another stage was borrowed. Preserve the original preset.
2. Adapt the EQ to this recording. A modest low cut can address rumble; small upper-mid cuts can address bite. Retain body and intelligibility. Avoid carrying a preset's broad treble cut, narrow notches or makeup gain into an already-processed stem without a reason.
3. Use de-essing or dynamic EQ for sharp consonants and intermittent harshness. Tune its band, threshold/drive and timing against this voice. A partial slot blend can retain consonant detail. Confirm which bands actually compress and whether any master stage or saturation is active.
4. Adjust vocal/beat balance with headroom. This can avoid driving a limiter to make the voice audible. Keep the instrumental insert free of EQ or dynamics when the agreed task is vocal-only; output peak control acts on the combined mix and should be described separately.
5. Compare the same passage at matched volume, then provide the full song. Distinguish tone/dynamics changes from a louder vocal balance. Record user feedback and keep the approved full file.

Possible search regions include upper-mid bite around 2-6 kHz and sibilance around 4-10 kHz, but choose by the recording. Small EQ moves and partial de-esser blends are starting experiments, not fixed prescriptions. Do not assume the same threshold, crossover, gain or loudness target suits another singer or song.

## Preserve the result

Save to a separately named working project through the verified command route, reopen it, and check the audible slot states, mix values, plugin parameters and routing. Export a native vocal FST from that saved state if useful. Record whether only structural export or an actual preset reload was checked. The saved preset's fader level is song-specific and needs review on a new recording.

Render the full arrangement in saved Song mode. Verify expected duration, complete decoding, format, loudness and true peak. Keep the full master distinct from matched-volume snippets and document any constant finishing trim applied after FL rendering.
